FIELD 2 — PROJECT SUMMARY (132 Words)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fire behaves deceptively in microgravity. Without buoyant natural convection, flames form invisible, spherical low-temperature shells that produce lethal carbon monoxide and survive unexpectedly at low oxygen thresholds. IGNIS-0G is a spacecraft fire safety intelligence system built for deep-space mission verification. Powered by a real-time 60 FPS Navier-Stokes and chemical kinetics aerodynamics simulator, it accurately predicts flame morphology, buoyancy forces, and the critical BASS 45-second extinction boundary across Earth, Mars, Moon, and ISS regimes. The platform integrates 17 verified flight experiments from NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) repository, an interactive NASA-STD-6001B spacecraft material risk matrix benchmarking SoFIE fine water mist versus toxic CO2 flooding, and a zero-hallucination multi-provider RAG AI analyst with deterministic offline TF-IDF fallback. IGNIS-0G equips aerospace engineers and astronauts with predictive fire suppression intelligence for Artemis and Mars exploration missions.

FIELD 3 — DETAILED PROJECT DESCRIPTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. THE CRITICAL SPACECRAFT FIRE HAZARD
Combustion in zero gravity diverges fundamentally from terrestrial physics:
- In 1.0g Earth gravity, buoyant convection accelerates hot gases upward (Grashof number Gr > 10^6), generating the familiar flickering teardrop flame that naturally entrains fresh oxidizer while dispersing combustion products.
- In 0.0g microgravity (ISS / Gateway), buoyancy collapses entirely (Fb -> 0, Gr -> 0). Combustion becomes purely diffusion-limited. In stagnant cabin air (0 cm/s airflow), burning fuels quickly suffocate within an envelope of their own CO2 and H2O combustion products (the BASS Draft Paradox).
- Paradoxically, gentle spacecraft life-support ventilation (2.5 to 5 cm/s) revitalizes combustion, while higher speeds can quench it.
- Furthermore, experiments like NASA FLEX discovered invisible "cool flames" (550–800 K) supported by peroxy radical chain branching that burn silently without soot, emitting lethal concentrations of carbon monoxide (CO).
- Under exploration baselines like the Artemis Exploration Atmosphere (34% O2 at 56.5 kPa / 8.2 psia), terrestrial flammability limits no longer apply. Materials deemed self-extinguishing on Earth can burn vigorously on Lunar and Martian habitats.

2. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & SCIENTIFIC METHODOLOGY
IGNIS-0G provides an end-to-end mission verification and telemetry platform divided into five tightly integrated modules:

A. 60 FPS Microgravity Combustion & Aerodynamics Simulator
- Native HTML5 Canvas particle physics engine running at an unthrottled 60 FPS.
- Couples real-time Navier-Stokes airflow field advection, oxygen diffusion kinetics, soot radiation, and chemical quenching.
- Dynamic telemetry calculations updated at 60 Hz:
  * Buoyancy Force: Fb = (rho_ambient - rho_flame) * g * V
  * Grashof Number: Gr = (g * beta * (T_flame - T_ambient) * L^3) / nu^2
  * Froude Number: Fr = U / sqrt(g * L)
  * Reynolds Number: Re = (U * L) / nu
  * Radiant Heat Loss fraction and toxic CO offgassing (ppm)
- Models the BASS 45-second zero-flow suffocation countdown and FLEX quasi-steady cool flame chemistry.

B. NASA Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) Curated Dataset
- 17 verified, peer-reviewed flight and ground experiments curated directly from NASA PSI and NASA Glenn Research Center databases.
- Spans 7 major NASA spaceflight combustion programs:
  1. Saffire (Spacecraft Fire Safety Demonstration Project on Cygnus vehicles)
  2. FLEX / FLEX-2 (Flame Extinguishment Experiment aboard ISS CIR)
  3. BASS / BASS-II (Burning and Suppression of Solids)
  4. ACME (Advanced Combustion via Microgravity Experiments)
  5. SoFIE (Solid Fuel Ignition and Extinction)
  6. BRE (Burning Rate Emulator)
  7. SLICE (Smoke and Line Carbon Emission)
- Full client-side export to formatted JSON and CSV datasets.

C. Spacecraft Material Risk Matrix & NASA-STD-6001B Benchmark
- Multi-atmosphere flammability evaluation engine covering 8 standardized spacecraft materials (PMMA/Plexiglas, Nomex, Kapton, Silicone RTV, PTFE/Teflon, Polyethylene, Polyurethane Foam, Cotton/Cellulose).
- Four pre-configured operational flight profiles:
  * ISS / Gateway Nominal (21% O2, 101.3 kPa, 0.0g)
  * Artemis Exploration Atmosphere (34% O2, 56.5 kPa, 0.16g)
  * Orion / Transit Contingency (26.5% O2, 70.3 kPa, 0.0g)
  * Mars Surface Habitat (26% O2, 70.3 kPa, 0.38g)
- NASA-STD-6001B Test 1 flammability prediction with Maximum Oxygen Concentration (MOC) thresholds.
- Fire suppression benchmark: Quantitative comparison between NASA SoFIE Fine Water Mist (2.26 MJ/kg latent heat quenching, zero settling, closed-loop ECLSS recovery) and legacy CO2 flooding (hypercapnia hazard, LiOH canister depletion).

D. Cross-Experiment Telemetry Correlation & Anomaly Graph
- Interactive multi-axis Recharts scatter charts mapping Flame Spread Rate (mm/s) vs. Oxygen Concentration (%).
- Airflow velocity vs. combustion sustainability profiles highlighting the 2.5–15 cm/s forced convection survival window in zero gravity.
- Documented flight anomalies: BASS zero-flow extinction, FLEX cool flame longevity, and SLICE high CO soot super-aggregation.

E. Zero-Hallucination Multi-Provider AI Flight Safety Analyst
- Universal RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) engine strictly grounded in the 17 NASA PSI flight records.
- Deterministic Offline TF-IDF Engine requiring zero API keys, zero network, and zero external dependencies.
- Universal plug-and-play support for cloud AI models: Google Gemini 2.0, OpenAI GPT-4o, Anthropic Claude 3.5, and Custom OpenAI-compatible endpoints (Groq, Ollama, DeepSeek).
- RAM-only API key handling: zero localStorage/cookie exposure, strictly memory-isolated.
